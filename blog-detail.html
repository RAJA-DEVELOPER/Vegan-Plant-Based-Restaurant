Eating with the Seasons:
A Guide to Autumn Plant-Based Cooking

Chef Marco Viani | November 15, 2026 | 8 min read | 24 Comments
Tweet Share Pin Copied!

As the days shorten and the air carries that first bite of autumn chill, the kitchen at LeafTable transforms into something altogether more warming and deeply satisfying. Gone are the abundant summer tomatoes and courgettes; in their place arrive the magnificent root vegetables, brassicas, wild mushrooms and squashes that define this most generous of seasons. At LeafTable, we don't fight the seasons — we surrender to them completely, allowing what our partner farms bring us each Thursday morning to dictate the week's creative menu.

Why Seasonal Cooking Matters More Than Ever

Seasonal cooking is not merely a culinary trend — it is the most honest relationship food can have with the environment. When an ingredient is grown in its natural season, it reaches peak nutritional density, peak flavour complexity, and requires the least energy-intensive agricultural intervention. At LeafTable, our sourcing decisions are entirely seasonal: we commit to never purchasing a vegetable out of its natural growing season, even if it means adapting our menu week by week. This requires genuine creativity and a deep knowledge of what our 24 partner farms can offer at any given moment. The result is a menu that feels alive — always changing, always surprising, always at its very best.

"A vegetable grown in its natural season and harvested at peak ripeness contains more nutrients, more flavour and more life than anything grown in a polytunnel in January. The earth knows what it's doing — we just have to listen." — Chef Marco Viani, Head Chef & Founder

Autumn's Star Ingredients This Season

This autumn has gifted us an extraordinary harvest. Here are the five ingredients that are defining our menu right now — and how we're using them:

  1. 1
    Heritage Cauliflower — No longer a side dish. In purple, orange and romanesco forms, heritage cauliflower delivers extraordinary visual drama and natural sweetness when roasted whole. We serve ours as a main course steak — seared, blackened at the edges, magnificent.
  2. 2
    Celeriac — Knobbly and unassuming outside, this root reveals a silky celery-scented interior when roasted or puréed. It forms our seasonal velouté base and pairs perfectly with truffle oil.
  3. 3
    Wild Mushrooms — Sussex woodlands supply us weekly with porcini, chanterelle and oyster mushrooms. The umami depth they bring to plant-based cooking is completely unmatched by anything else in the larder.
  4. 4
    Butternut Squash — Sweet, abundant and deeply versatile. We roast the flesh for risotto, toast the seeds for salad garnish, and dehydrate the skin as a savoury crisp topping.
  5. 5
    Purple Kale — Grown on our rooftop garden above the restaurant, purple kale is at its sweetest after the first frost. Massaged with lemon oil, it becomes the finest salad leaf of the season.
Pro Tip from Chef Marco: "Always taste your vegetables raw first — this tells you everything you need to know about how to treat them in the kitchen. A sweet parsnip wants very little done to it; a bitter radicchio needs balancing. Listen to the ingredient."

Three Recipes to Try at Home This Weekend

These are home-cook adaptations of dishes from our current menu — simplified for your kitchen without sacrificing any of the flavour or soul. Each serves four people.

Butternut Squash & Sage Risotto

⏱ Prep: 15 min Cook: 35 min Serves: 4
Ingredients:
  • 1 large butternut squash, peeled and diced
  • 300g arborio rice
  • 1.2L hot vegetable stock
  • 1 large white onion, finely diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 100ml dry white wine (or extra stock)
  • 2 tbsp nutritional yeast
  • Fresh sage leaves, 2 tbsp olive oil, salt, white pepper
Method:
  1. Roast squash at 200°C for 25 minutes until golden. Reserve half as chunks; purée the rest with a splash of stock until silky.
  2. Sauté onion and garlic in olive oil over medium heat for 8 minutes. Add rice, toast 2 minutes, deglaze with wine.
  3. Add hot stock ladle by ladle, stirring constantly, until each addition is absorbed — about 22 minutes total. Fold in squash purée, roasted chunks, nutritional yeast and seasoning. Fry sage in hot oil until crisp and serve immediately on top.

Roasted Root Vegetable Tray Bake with Tahini Dressing

⏱ Prep: 10 min Cook: 40 min Serves: 4
Ingredients:
  • 2 large carrots, 2 parsnips, 1 small celeriac, 1 red onion, 200g beetroot (all diced 2cm)
  • 3 tbsp olive oil, 2 tsp cumin, 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp ground coriander, salt and pepper
  • Dressing: 3 tbsp tahini, 1 lemon juiced, 1 garlic clove grated, 4–6 tbsp cold water, pinch of salt
Method:
  1. Toss all veg in oil and spices. Roast at 200°C for 40 mins, turning once halfway through.
  2. Whisk tahini, lemon, garlic and water together until pourable and smooth.
  3. Serve roasted veg on a bed of peppery rocket, with tahini dressing drizzled generously over the top.

Apple & Cinnamon Overnight Oats

⏱ Prep: 5 min Overnight Serves: 2
Ingredients:
  • 160g rolled oats
  • 400ml oat milk
  • 2 tbsp chia seeds
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 2 tbsp maple syrup
  • 2 eating apples
Method:
  1. Mix oats, milk, chia, cinnamon and maple syrup in a jar or container. Stir well to combine.
  2. Cover and refrigerate overnight (minimum 6 hours).
  3. In the morning, top with grated apple, a sprinkle of cinnamon, and toasted pecans if you have them.

Seasonal cooking is ultimately an act of trust — trust in nature's intelligence, trust in our farmers, and trust in the idea that the best ingredient is the one that grew when and where it was meant to. At LeafTable, every dish we serve is an expression of that trust. We hope these recipes bring a little of that philosophy into your own kitchen this autumn. If you make any of these dishes at home, we'd genuinely love to see them — tag us @leaftable on Instagram and we'll share our favourites. Until next time, eat well, eat seasonally, and eat with joy.

Chef Marco trained at Le Cordon Bleu Paris before working in Michelin-starred kitchens across London and Florence.
